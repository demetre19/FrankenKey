# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Android users who want a fast everyday keyboard, an optional dense coding keyboard, and a private on-device Reader for saved articles and documents. They use FrankenKey in mobile, accessibility, reading, messaging, and technical workflows where reliable controls and low visual clutter matter.

## Product Purpose

FrankenKey combines efficient text input, local adaptive assistance, clipboard tools, and a focused reading experience without ads or tracking. Core typing and reading remain local; optional Reader AI sends only an explicitly selected URL-imported article to OpenRouter after disclosure, using the user's own encrypted API key.

## Brand Personality

Private, capable, uncluttered.

## Anti-references

- Cluttered mobile interfaces that expose every advanced action at once.
- Decorative controls or novelty interactions that obscure standard Android behavior.
- Reading surfaces that prioritize chrome over article content.
- Product descriptions or UI that depend on comparisons with competing brands or device manufacturers.

## Design Principles

- Keep user content primary and interface chrome secondary.
- Use familiar Android affordances and explicit feedback for consequential actions.
- Preserve privacy boundaries and local ownership of personal data.
- Earn density through utility; hide optional complexity until it is relevant.
- Keep text visibly inset: bordered inputs and learned-word rows reserve at least 12dp between text and their inner horizontal edges.
- Make reading and typing controls accessible, predictable, and comfortable on small screens.
- Treat vocabulary as intentional personal data: learn words only through explicit teaching or a deliberate correction choice after editing that word, never from ordinary typing or automatic autocorrection.
- Keep cloud features explicit and scoped: Reader AI is URL-article-only, preserves raw outputs locally, and shares nothing further without a user action.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use high-contrast text and controls, descriptive accessibility labels, scalable system text, and at least 48dp touch targets for icon actions. Preserve standard Back behavior, avoid decorative motion, and ensure reading media supports direct enlargement, panning, and zooming without requiring precise taps.
